Zero Finder

|η(σ+it)| swept across the critical strip. Watch what a real zero looks like — and what a generic height looks like instead.

η(s) = ∑ (−1)n+1 n−s

Height t

    |η(½+it)|, on the line
    height t

    Each curve traces |η(σ+it)| as σ runs from 0 to 1, at a fixed height t. At a real, known zero, the curve dives toward zero exactly at σ = ½ (marked). At an ordinary height, picked for having nothing special about it, the curve doesn't dip toward zero anywhere along the line at all — there's simply nothing there. The whole rest of this series is about why that dip, when it happens, only ever happens at σ = ½.
